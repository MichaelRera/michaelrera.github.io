SmurfTrsc for FlyBase

Smurf vs non-Smurf gene expression right on FlyBase pages. A browser extension for Chrome and Chromium-based browsers (Firefox version under review).

Reading a FlyBase page and wondering how the gene behaves as flies age? SmurfTrsc for FlyBase shows its expression in the whole-body transcriptome of Smurf and non-Smurf Drosophila melanogaster (Zane et al., 2023) directly on FlyBase report pages, so you can tell a change tied to the Smurf phase from a change with age without leaving the page you are reading.

SmurfTrsc for FlyBase in under 30 seconds: Thor, flat with age but higher in Smurfs; the samples one by one, the age contrasts and the table of values; then Gclc, reached through FlyBase's Jump to Gene box.

What it shows

On FlyBase gene (FBgn), protein (FBpp), transcript (FBtr) and allele (FBal) pages, a panel at the top of the report gives, for the gene:

  • A one-line verdict. Up, down or no change in Smurfs, with the log2 fold change and adjusted p, and the paper’s category: Smurf gene, old non-Smurf gene, or both.
  • All 32 samples. A strip plot by age (20, 30 and 40 days) and Smurf status, with group means. Hover a sample for its details, or open the table of all values.
  • Smurf status and age, side by side. The paper’s three DESeq2 contrasts: Smurfs vs non-Smurfs, and 40 vs 20 days within each status.
  • The rest of the paper’s results for the gene, where it has them: longevity candidates, the linear model on expression, progressive change in non-Smurfs, proteomics, GenAge lifespan effects and the Human Ageing Atlas ortholog.
Thor (4E-BP): low in non-Smurfs and high in Smurfs at 20, 30 and 40 days, with no change with age within either group. The two phases of ageing, in one gene.
Gclc: up in Smurfs and also in old non-Smurfs. Hovering a sample gives its age and the time since its Smurf transition; the table lists all 32 values.

Where the data come from

The panel shows the published results of Zane et al. (2023): whole-body RNA-seq of mated Drs-GFP females, Smurf and non-Smurf, at 20, 30 and 40 days, analysed with DESeq2 (significant at adjusted p ≤ 0.05, as in the paper). Genes are matched to the current FlyBase release; genes renamed or merged since the paper’s annotation are flagged in the panel. The data come from the paper’s repository, SmurfsTrsc, and ship inside the extension.

It works alongside ProteoCast for FlyBase: with both installed, the two panels sit one above the other.

A protein page, Thor-PA, with both extensions: ProteoCast's mutational sensitivity, then the gene's Smurf transcriptome.

Install

SmurfTrsc for FlyBase works in Chrome, Edge, Brave and other Chromium-based browsers. A Firefox version is under review at Firefox Add-ons.

Add to Chrome — Chrome Web Store

  • Chrome Web Store: SmurfTrsc for FlyBase: one click, updates automatically. Edge and Brave users can install it from the same page (Edge first asks to allow extensions from other stores).
  • Firefox Add-ons: under review.
  • Source code: github.com/MichaelRera/smurftrsc-flybase (open source, GPL-3.0, coded with Claude Code; bug reports and suggestions welcome as GitHub issues).

Privacy

The extension reads only the FlyBase page you are on (the identifier in its address and, on allele pages, the associated gene). The data ship inside it: it makes no network requests at all. No analytics, no tracking, nothing collected.
